Complex Numbers

Class XI — Practice Test • Solutions & Value-Point Marking Scheme

Class XIComplex NumbersSections A–DTeacher-led practice

SECTION A — 1 Mark Each

1. Equate real and imaginary parts.
1 mark
Step I
\(4x=3\Rightarrow x=\frac34\), and \(3x-y=-6\Rightarrow y=\frac{33}{4}\).
Correct Option: (b)
2. Find the multiplicative inverse of \(z=a+ib\).
1 mark
Step I
\(z^{-1}=\dfrac{\bar z}{|z|^2}=\dfrac{a-ib}{a^2+b^2}\).
Correct Option: (a)
3. Use conjugates and the modulus identity.
1 mark
Step I
\((z+3)(\bar z+3)=z\bar z+3(z+\bar z)+9\), and \(|z+3|^2=(z+3)(\bar z+3)\).
Thus, both (a) and (b) are correct.
Correct Option: (c)
4. Apply equality of two complex numbers.
1 mark
Step I
By definition, for \(a+ib=c+id\), the real and imaginary parts are separately equal: \(a=c\) and \(b=d\).
Correct Option: (b)
5. Evaluate \(i^{45}+i^{-69}\).
1 mark
Step I
\(i^{45}=(i^4)^{11}i=i\), while \(i^{-69}=\frac1{i^{69}}=-i\).
Hence \(i+(-i)=0\).
Correct Option: (a)
6. Recall the inverse formula.
1 mark
Step I
\(z^{-1}=\dfrac{\bar z}{|z|^2}\).
Correct Option: (b)
7. Simplify \(\frac{1+i}{1-i}\) and determine the least positive \(n\) for \(i^n=1\).
1 mark
Step I
\(\frac{1+i}{1-i}=i\). Also \(i^n=1\) first occurs at \(n=4\).
Correct Option: (b)
8. Use the condition for collinearity of three points.
1 mark
Step I
Area \(=\frac12|2(4-2)+0(2-3)+p(3-4)|=0\Rightarrow4-p=0\Rightarrow p=4\).
Correct Option: (a)
9. Given \(|z|=1\), simplify the required expression.
1 mark
Step I
Since \(|z|=1\Rightarrow z\bar z=1\Rightarrow\bar z=1/z\). Substitution gives the required value \(z\).
Correct Option: (a)
10. For \(z=1+2i\), evaluate the modulus of the given rational expression.
1 mark
Step I — ½
\(z^2=(1+2i)^2=-3+4i\). Also \(7-z=6-2i\), so \(|7-z|=\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}\).
Step II — ½
\(1+z^2=-2+4i\), so \(|1+z^2|=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5\). Hence \(\frac{|7-z|}{|1+z^2|}=\frac{2\sqrt{10}}{2\sqrt5}=\sqrt2\).
Correct Option: (a)

SECTION B — 2 Marks Each

11. Simplify each power and evaluate the expression.
2 marks
Step I — 1
\(i^{19}=i^{16}i^3=-i\), and \(\left(\frac1i\right)^{25}=(-i)^{25}=-i\).
Step II — 1
\([-i+(-i)]^2=(-2i)^2=-4\).
12. Find \(1+i^2+i^4+i^6+\cdots+i^{20}\).
2 marks
Step I — 1
Using \(i^2=-1\), the 11 terms are \(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots,1\).
Step II — 1
\(S_{11}=\frac{1(1-(-1)^{11})}{1-(-1)}=1\).
13. Solve \(|1-i|^x=2^x\) in integers and count the non-zero solutions.
2 marks
Step I — 1
\(|1-i|^x=(\sqrt2)^x=2^x\).
Step II — 1
Thus \(2^{x/2}=2^x\Rightarrow x=0\). Therefore, the number of non-zero integral solutions is \(0\).

SECTION C — 3 Marks Each

14. Prove the required identity involving four complex factors.
3 marks
Step I — 1
Take modulus of both sides: \(|(a+ib)(c+id)(e+if)(g+ih)|=|A+iB|\).
Step II — 1
Apply \(|z_1z_2|=|z_1||z_2|\).
Step III — 1
Squaring gives \((a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2)(e^2+f^2)(g^2+h^2)=A^2+B^2\). Hence proved.
15. If \((x+iy)^3=u+iv\), prove the required relation.
3 marks
Step I — 1
Expanding, \(u=x^3-3xy^2\) and \(v=3x^2y-y^3\).
Step II — 1
Therefore \(\frac ux=x^2-3y^2\) and \(\frac vy=3x^2-y^2\).
Step III — 1
Adding gives \(\frac ux+\frac vy=4(x^2-y^2)\). Hence proved.
16. Solve the complex equation involving a modulus.
3 marks
Step I — 1
Let \(z=x+iy\). Substitution gives \((x+2\sqrt{(x+1)^2+y^2})+i(y+1)=0\).
Step II — 1
Imaginary part: \(y+1=0\Rightarrow y=-1\).
Step III — 1
The real part gives \(x+2\sqrt{x^2+2x+2}=0\). With \(x\le0\), squaring yields \((x+2)^2=0\Rightarrow x=-2\). Therefore \(z=-2-i\).

SECTION D — 5 Marks

17. Prove the modulus identity for \(w=\left|\frac{\beta-\alpha}{1-\bar\alpha\beta}\right|\), where \(|\alpha|=|\beta|=1\).
5 marks
Step I — 1.5
\(w^2=\frac{(\beta-\alpha)(\bar\beta-\bar\alpha)}{(1-\bar\alpha\beta)(1-\alpha\bar\beta)}\).
Step II — 2
Using \(|\alpha|=|\beta|=1\), the numerator and denominator simplify to the same expression.
Step III — 1.5
Numerator = Denominator \(\Rightarrow w^2=1\Rightarrow w=1\), since \(w\ge0\). Hence proved.
OR. Alternative Choice Question Solution.
5 marks
Step I — 2
Take modulus of both sides of \(x+iy=\sqrt{\frac{a+ib}{c+id}}\): \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=\sqrt{\frac{|a+ib|}{|c+id|}}\).
Step II — 1.5
Squaring gives \(x^2+y^2=\frac{a^2+b^2}{c^2+d^2}\).
Step III — 1.5
Squaring again gives \((x^2+y^2)^2=\frac{a^2+b^2}{c^2+d^2}\). Hence proved.
